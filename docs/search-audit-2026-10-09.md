## Search and Inclusion Method

The recent window was October 4–9. Queries covered μP / muP / μTransfer / maximal-update variants, Tensor Programs, coordinate checks, proxy-to-target transfer, optimizer-specific scaling, optimal learning rate, batch-size invariance, Hyperball / AdamH / MuonH, and their author resources. Backward checks followed references in batch-scaling and data-constrained-training papers, official author and conference pages, framework documentation, the community μP index, and source-code discussions.

Primary methods and relevant appendices were inspected before including a paper. Source files and author data were checked for implementation provenance; training sweeps were not rerun. The strict μP and Hyperball collections require substantive method, analysis, validation, or application evidence. Complementary HPT may include a narrow theoretical optimum or a study that falsifies a scale rule, but not performance-only scaling or ordinary cross-dataset transfer. Embedded hyperparameter studies are described at their actual scope.

Dates distinguish first verified public release from revision and venue year. TACO is first public October 1, with reviewed v2 on October 6; Graph Metanetworks is September 30, not its October 7 news-summary date. Kolibri's official release is October 3 and has no verified arXiv identifier. The batch-size paper uses its verified October 6 arXiv date: its author-PDF Git history is dated October 2, but repository creation and commit timestamps do not establish the exact earlier public posting day.

## Newly Included μP Papers and Reports

| Paper | Primary evidence and demonstrated boundary |
|---|---|
| [How to scale your HEP ML models](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06784v1) | Section 5.1 / Figure 8 independently checks width and learning rate in jet-tagging Transformers. Complete(d)P supports width transfer, but depth drift requires an additional fitted exponent 0.46, approximated by one-half. Later batch / horizon fits do not make the uncorrected depth rule universally invariant. First public October 5. |
| [Kolibri: A Sovereign European Model on the Pareto Frontier](https://aleph-alpha.com/downloads/tech-report.pdf) | Sections 2.2.1.2–3 / Figure 10 validate approximate μP width transfer from 1024 to 2048 at a common token budget, with a one-grid-step, approximately √2 optimum drift. Production-depth proxies and cumulative-learning-rate forecasts select Muon learning rate, batch and decay for a 20T-token recipe; internal forecast-validation curves are not shown and the production optimum is not independently swept. The [official October 3 announcement](https://aleph-alpha.com/en/blog/kolibri-has-landed-a-sovereign-open-weight-model/) links the report. |
| [TACO](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02199v2) | Section 4 / Theorem 4 derives spectral update scaling under balanced row-winner counts. Appendix B.3 independently sweeps OPT and Qwen3 full-parameter fine-tuning scales, reporting a shared optimum at 1,000 steps, batch 4 and five seeds. This is conditional post-training evidence, not an unconditional pretraining guarantee or isolated width-only experiment. |
| [Transferable Graph Metanetworks](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00420v1) | Sections 4–5 and Appendices D.3 / E use μP-trained weight populations as a central control, tune INR learning rate once at base width 24/32, and check fits up to width 1024. The size-generalization guarantees concern μP-trained MLP inputs with fixed finite-horizon / optimizer / data laws; CNN results are empirical. |

## Seven Complementary HPT Omissions Recovered

| Paper | Qualifying hyperparameter result and limitation |
|---|---|
| [Prescriptive Scaling Laws for Data Constrained Training](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01640v1#S6) | Section 6 / Figure 8 / Table 2 fit separate weight-decay 0.1 and 1.0 regimes, predict their compute crossover, and test held-out configurations. Included for this embedded two-value decay-selection study, not a continuous decay optimum or general transfer law. First public May 2, 2026. |
| [Pre-training under infinite compute](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14786v1#S4.SS2) | Small ensembles estimate large-ensemble loss asymptotes and hyperparameter rankings. Appendix C.2 tests a heuristic retaining learning rate, doubling epochs and halving decay across displayed model sizes, with an explicit 1.4B / 200M exception. Infinite-ensemble optima are inferred rather than measured. First public September 18, 2025; ICLR 2026. |
| [Batch size invariant Adam](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18824v1) | A microbatch-second-moment variant combines linear learning-rate and moment-refresh scaling to approximate batch invariance in the small-update limit, with CIFAR-10 tests. This is not a universal rule for standard Adam or exact finite-step invariance. First public February 29, 2024. |
| [Batch size-invariance for policy optimization](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/6ceb6c2150bbf46fd75528a6cd6be793-Abstract.html) | PPO / PPG-EWMA decouple behavior and proximal policies and coordinate optimizer, EWMA, advantage and phase settings. Procgen tests preserve learning approximately across large batch changes under stated small-optimization-batch conditions. First public October 1, 2021; NeurIPS 2022. |
| [On the Validity of Modeling SGD with Stochastic Differential Equations](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.12470v2) | Section 5.2 / Theorem 5.6 give a gradient/noise diagnostic for failure of linear batch / learning-rate scaling in scale-invariant networks. SDE validity is sufficient, not necessary, for the scaling rule. First public February 24, 2021. |
| [AdaScale SGD](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/johnson20a.html) | Online gradient-variance estimates adapt the learning rate and gain-based training clock from a baseline SGD schedule to larger batches. Its convergence statements retain their assumptions; the framework integration is not the original experiment release. First public July 9, 2020. |
| [Measuring the Effects of Data Parallelism on Neural Network Training](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v20/18-789.html) | Independently tuned learning rate, momentum and schedules across 35 workloads show linear / square-root rules do not consistently recover batch-dependent step-to-target optima. This qualifies as direct falsification, not a universal replacement law. First public November 8, 2018; JMLR 2019. |

## Hyperball Paper, Source, and Version Checks

[The Best Optimizer Depends on Batch Size](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08975) is a direct Hyperball paper: Section 4 / Figure 3 and Appendix F independently retune AdamH, LionH, MuonH, KL-SOAP-H and ShampooH across 128K–2M tokens per update. Optimizer rankings change even under matrix norm control. Most Hyperball endpoints are single-seed, and near-equal losses should not be described as statistically resolved wins. The author blog and measured-data / interactive artifact are added separately from the paper, with no complete training-code release independently verified.

The full current [Hyperball audit is included in the root README](../README.md#hyperball-incremental-audit--2026-10-09). An August sharded-norm failure / repair investigation is recovered as primary implementation evidence, not a new October experiment or a local reproduction.

The eight newest existing complementary HPT arXiv histories were also checked, with no October 4–9 revisions found. The newest existing μP hybrid-architecture and growing-horizon records remain v1. ## Exclusions and Access Limits

- [Su Jianlin's tutorial](https://kexue.fm/archives/10770), [Trisham Patil's note](https://trishampatil.com/ai%20engineering/2026/09/06/mup-maximal-update-parameterization-hyperparameter-transfer/), [Diego's CompleteP note](https://dmartinezt.github.io/research_notes/nn_parametrization/completep.html), and [Mohak Chugh's μP / unit-scaling essay](https://mohakchugh.is-a.dev/blog/mup-unit-scaling-hyperparameter-transfer-fp8-training/) remain uncounted leads where full direct-body retrieval failed. Search snippets are not full-source verification.
- The [JSSR student abstract](https://journals.gmu.edu/jssr/article/view/5262) is titled as an SP / μP comparison, but its accessible record does not supply a full manuscript or substantive μP results. It remains an abstract-only candidate, not a verified direct paper.
- [Laz4rz/mup](https://github.com/Laz4rz/mup/blob/b8983141004c64d236fcfa9bfe0be5c0afc18b52/train_mlp.py) has a consistent all-parameter SGD path, but its inspected Adam rates do not match the stated forward-coordinate μP scaling. It is not promoted as a verified μP implementation without a documented convention resolving that discrepancy.
- [Nanochat PR #644](https://github.com/karpathy/nanochat/pull/644) remains unmerged. Its author reports flat coordinate checks but later reports worse target results than SP after retuning and suspects proxy / horizon mismatch; that is not proof of a specific failure cause. Its explanation also incorrectly treats a polar matrix's Frobenius norm as dimension-independent. It is not an authoritative recipe or independently rerun transfer result.
- [Does Muon Need Fine-Grained Spectral Shaping?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07497) does not establish a substantive μP / scale-transfer result in the inspected body. [A Riemannian Geometry for Low-rank Adaptation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08049) uses different ranks across tasks but supplies no qualifying rank-scale hyperparameter-transfer rule. Generic geometric optimization is not automatically μP or HPT.
- The Kolibri secondary tutorial was only a discovery lead; inclusion follows the official report. Generic HyperBall graph-centrality / packing names, optimizer aliases without sphere retraction, mirrors and automatic summaries remain excluded. Some OpenReview pages returned browser challenges; the live Hyperball blog and oversized arXiv HTML required author-source or PDF alternatives.
