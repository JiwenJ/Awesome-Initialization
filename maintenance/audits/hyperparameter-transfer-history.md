# Scale-Aware HPT Maintenance History

Historical evidence and maintenance notes moved from the HPT guide. Dates and source-access statements retain their original cutoffs.

## Evidence Limits and Maintenance

The public-source audit covers records available through **2026-10-20**. It searched exact hyperparameter-transfer terminology, learning-rate and batch-size scaling, model/data/token/sparsity laws, parameterization methods, normalized and hypersphere training, citations in recent papers, and official code releases. The latest pass adds Microsoft's October 15 frontier-pretraining report, plus backward omissions on video diffusion, encoder pretraining, feature-learning-strength scaling, Opt-Laws and learning-rate annealing. The October 15 pass added four October studies on depth transfer, cooldown/horizon laws, repeated-data batch rules, and multi-step RLVR, plus three critical-batch omissions. Narrow theory, embedded sections and asymptotic extrapolations are labeled at their demonstrated scope; neither a fitted optimum nor a successful transferred run proves universal or zero-shot transfer. Indexed search cannot prove absolute completeness, and several 2026 items remain preprints.

Microsoft's report provides an embedded empirical recipe with small-to-intermediate validation and a completed frontier application; it does not release a target-scale optimum sweep or enough sweep data to independently refit its laws. The encoder study's public proceedings date is verified only to November 2025. The feature-learning-strength paper was excluded from strict μP in the September audit because its construction is analogous to μP; its separate width/data output-multiplier study qualifies for this complementary collection. Opt-Laws is reviewed at v4 (May 20, 2026), and the annealing paper at the NeurIPS 2025 proceedings version. Their schedule-selection claims are empirical: the annealing paper specifically warns that unconstrained mathematical optimization of its approximate equation can yield collapsed schedules. No new author experiment-code release is counted for these additions.

The depth-smoothness paper's primary arXiv record lists only v1 on October 9; a secondary site's October 14 update is not treated as a verified manuscript revision. The [Two Clocks author-code URL](https://github.com/WinstonLiyt/two-clocks-rlvr) returned 404 during this audit and is not counted as a verified artifact. The least-squares schedule code is verified research software, but its asymptotic formulas and heuristic critical-batch threshold are not established transfer rules for AdamW or LLMs. The repeated-data paper's GPU-time estimates and 70B/10T recipe are fitted projections, not measured large-scale training.

The earlier adaptive-SDE version is dated May 2024 on OpenReview; its exact public day could not be verified, so the table retains month-level precision and separately states the arXiv date. ScAn-Bench's manuscript and author repository list eight authors, while the arXiv abstract metadata omits Steven Adriaensen; the bibliography follows the manuscript. The infinite-compute paper's detailed transfer scope was reviewed from arXiv v1; its ICLR 2026 status is verified from the primary conference PDF record. Promised but unreleased code and evaluation-only artifacts are not counted as hyperparameter-transfer implementations. Released measurements and plot code are labeled as artifacts, not complete training implementations.
